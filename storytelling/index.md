BUT WHAT FONT ACTUALLY IS?

![Slide](img/FONTS.011.jpeg)

Let’s start from the Typography ~ it’s a field that deals with an art and technique of arranging type ~ that means that letters, words, and paragraphs are styled and placed in a way that’s readable and looks good.

![Slide](img/FONTS.012.jpeg)

Then we have typefaces or font families if you want ~ that’s the overall design of a set of characters. Like Arial, or it can be f.e. Times New Roman, Helvetica, and many more ~ those are all typefaces.

And then we have the font itself, which is a specific style within a typeface ~ like this example, Arial in Bold and size 90.

![Slide](img/FONTS.013.jpeg)

You might be interested where it all begins? From where these fonts actually come?

![Slide](img/FONTS.026.jpeg)

Of course, they were made by designers, typographers, calligraphers and people who worked with writing systems ~ but the story goes much further back ~ around 10,000 years ago in ancient Mesopotamia.

![Slide](img/FONTS.027.jpeg)

Back then, people traded goods ~ as a example ~ someone wanted to trade his chicken for a someones goat, but what he actually wanted in the end was f. e. an olive oil ~ so that means that they just couldn’t make the trade immediately. And they needed a system to represent the things that was being exchanged.

![Slide](img/FONTS.028.jpeg)

So they made a system called CLAY BULLAE (bulé)...

![Slide](img/FONTS.029.jpeg)

... which were big clay symbols representing various things. 

![Slide](img/FONTS.030.jpeg)

They had them in about 16 categories, and each clay piece represented some real thing ~ 

![Slide](img/FONTS.031.jpeg)

~ like a chicken or a goat ~ instead of carrying the real thing around.

This idea ~ of representing things with symbols ~ is what led to the invention of Cuneiform.

![Slide](img/FONTS.032.jpeg)

Cuneiform ~ What is it?

Let’s take a look at this:

![Slide](img/FONTS.033.jpeg)

* This is an actual fish,

![Slide](img/FONTS.034.jpeg)

* this is a pictogram of a fish,

![Slide](img/FONTS.035.jpeg)

* and this is a cuneiform for fish.

![Slide](img/FONTS.036.jpeg)

So cuneiform is basically GRAPHIC SYMBOLS DEPICTING THINGS. It’s also one of the earliest forms of a formal written language.

![Slide](img/FONTS.037.jpeg)

Later, this idea evolved into famous alphabets like Egyptian hieroglyphs.

![Slide](img/FONTS.038.jpeg)

And now fast-forward a bit ~ we get to Ancient Greece. Where they simplified these symbols into 22 letters, carved into stone ~ which is called Greek lapidary letters.

![Slide](img/FONTS.039.jpeg)

 And from that point they could write many things ~ which was a huge step forward for communication.

![Slide](img/FONTS.040.jpeg)

Then came the Romans. They took the Greek letters and made them into what we call the Roman alphabet or Latin ~ the base of the alphabet we still use today.

![Slide](img/FONTS.041.jpeg)

And also they did one term of font ~ they added little hats and feet to the letters, and these are what we call ...

![Slide](img/FONTS.042.jpeg)

... SERIFS.

![Slide](img/FONTS.043.jpeg)

Now let’s talk about mass production. In 11th-century in China, was invented a movable type system ~ It was a typing machine made from ceramic pieces that let people print much faster and easier, and it also meant that they can mass produce messaging.

![Slide](img/FONTS.044.jpeg)

Then in 1440, a man named Johannes Gutenberg invented ...

![Slide](img/FONTS.045.jpeg)

... this machine ~ the printing press, that could mass produce lettering.

![Slide](img/FONTS.046.jpeg)

There we also can see the first ever printed book ~ The Gutenberg Bible with a first ever printed Roman typeface ~ 

![Slide](img/FONTS.047.jpeg)

![Slide](img/FONTS.049.jpeg)

And I personally realized this even more during one small client project, that I did some time ago.

I was contacted by an older woman, around 55 years old, and she wanted to build herself as a personal brand.

Her request was quite simple at first:
She wanted to see how her name could work as a logo.
She gave me a color palette, but when I asked her more questions ~ I didn’t get many clear answers.

She just told me that she is a therapist, she works with spiritual practices, the human body, holistic health, and things like this.
So I knew the field, but I didnt know the exact tone and nothing much about her or her preferences.

![Slide](img/FONTS.063.jpeg)

So I decided to make some visual possibilities and directions to show her that typography can completely change how the same message feels in different ways.

The first variant was elegant.
I used a very thin and decorative type, with soft details and a monogram I created from the initials K and M in Illustrator.
This version was meant to feel personal, feminine and sensitive.

The second variant was more modern.
Here I wanted to create something more readable, bold, and clear.
The typography became more geometric and simple. The brandmark was based more on a simplified symbol, almost like a small human figure or abstract body.
This version felt more confident and more digital. It could work better if the client wanted to look contemporary, practical, and accessible.

The third variant was organic.
This one was much more connected to nature, body, rituals, and spiritual practice.
The typography was tall, thin, and expressive. The monogram felt more like a symbol, almost like something between initials and a drawn mark.

The fourth variant was minimalistic.
More clean, balanced, and simple. The typography was elegant but not too decorative. The monogram was based also on the initials, but reduced into a clear and usable shape.

I think this version was probably the safest and most balanced direction.
It still felt personal and elegant, but it was easier to imagine across different brand materials ~ such as business cards, website, social media, printed materials. etc.

![Slide](img/FONTS.064.jpeg)

Unfortunately I didn’t get any feedback from her, but it it taught me that typography can become a tool for conversation, even when someone cannot explain exactly what he wants.
